Four weeks to Quiz 1

Quiz 1 tests the same skills as the weekly assignments, mostly as numbers you type in. I tagged every question from all 13 past Quiz 1 sittings (June 2022 to July 2026). Six patterns appear in 11 or 12 of the 13 papers: normalising a PDF, expectation from a PMF, max/min of i.i.d. variables, a probability from a PDF or CDF, variance, and Chebyshev. Week 3 is always worth 9–14 of the 40 marks. The plan below follows the course calendar and those patterns.

Deadlines that matter before the quiz. Weekly graded assignments are due on Sundays at 23:59 (Week 3: Sun 25 Oct); answers to past weeks are on the Graded assignments page once each deadline passes. To sit the end term you need the average of your best 5 of the first 7 weekly graded assignments to be at least 40/100, and you must attend at least one of the two quizzes. Final score: T = max(0.6F + 0.3·max(Qz1, Qz2), 0.45F + 0.25Qz1 + 0.3Qz2).

18–25 Oct · Phase 1

Weeks 1–2

  • Notes for Week 1, then Week 2
  • Practice Week 1 and Week 2 sets; redo every miss
  • Drills: joint table, conditional, Poisson split, max/min
  • Submit the Week 3 graded assignment by Sun 25 Oct

26 Oct–1 Nov · Phase 2

Weeks 3–4

  • Week 3 notes: E, Var, Cov, ρ, Markov, Chebyshev
  • Week 4 notes: CDF, PDF, uniform, exponential, normal
  • Watch the Week 4 lectures as they release on Seek
  • Practice Week 3 and Week 4 sets

2–8 Nov · Phase 3

Mixed practice

  • All worked examples, writing each step yourself
  • Past quiz patterns: drill every pattern above 50% chance
  • The predicted paper in exam mode, then review
  • Drills on your weakest topics (see the score on each)

9–14 Nov · Phase 4

Mocks and revision

  • Mock quizzes A, B and C, timed; a fresh predicted paper
  • Seek's own "Mock for Quiz 1" from the Jun 2026 run
  • Formula sheet from memory, then check
  • Traps list the night before

Sun 15 Nov · Quiz day

Light review only

  • Traps list and formula sheet, nothing new
  • Hall ticket and photo ID
  • Read every "correct to two decimal places" carefully
  • Check each PMF you build adds up to 1
