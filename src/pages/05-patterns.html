Past quiz patterns

I read and tagged every question from 13 Quiz 1 sittings (June 2022 to July 2026): 82 paper sets from quizpractice.space plus the answer-keyed Feb and Jun 2026 papers on Seek. Duplicate sets were merged, leaving the distinct questions below. Each one was tagged with the pattern it tests. Nothing here copies a question; it counts them.

What the data says

Six patterns are in almost every paper

Normalising a PDF (12 of 13 sittings), expectation from a PMF (12), max/min of i.i.d. variables (11), a probability from a PDF or CDF (11), variance (11) and Chebyshev (11). Master these six and you have roughly half the paper.

Seek's own mock is a real past paper

"Mock For Quiz 1" on the Jun 2026 Seek page is the Oct 2023 Quiz 1, question for question, and "Mock for Week 1 and 2" is mostly the Jun 2022 paper. Do both, but don't treat a good score there as a prediction.

Setups come back with new numbers

Exact repeats across sittings: none. Same setup, new numbers: often. Formula joint PMFs like (2x + y)/27, the "book store" pair of Poissons, U = XY from three coin tosses, a PDF drawn as a graph with α and β, and "roll a die, then toss that many coins" each appeared in two or more sittings.

The week split is steady

Week 3 is worth 9–14 marks in every paper. Week 4 is usually about 8 (range 5–14), and Weeks 1 and 2 share the rest. The chart below shows each sitting.

A fill-in-the-blanks set every time since Oct 2025

A worked solution with blanks A–E (1 or 0.5 mark each, about 3 marks in all): type the option number, then the final value. The tested topics were a conditional from a table, the max of two variables and P(X > a | X > b) from a PDF.

Traps the papers keep setting

Markov with a strict inequality on a count (P(X > 74) means P(X ≥ 75)) came up three times, once in reverse (find μ from the bound). Chebyshev is almost always "between a and b" around the mean. "Find k" before every Week 4 probability.
