Read on quiz morning

Last-minute traps

Small slips that cost marks in this course. Each line is a rule to apply, not a fact to recall.

Reading the question

  • "Correct to two decimal places": compute with full precision, round only at the end. 0.3636 → 0.36.
  • Given vs asked: in P(A | B) you divide by P(B), the condition.
  • Normal(μ, σ²) gives the variance. Normal(2, 5) has σ = √5 ≈ 2.236.
  • Exp(λ) uses the rate. "Mean 10 minutes" means λ = 0.1.
  • Geometric starts at 1. P(X > k) = (1 − p)^k, not (1 − p)^(k−1).
  • "Between a and b" for a continuous variable: ≤ or < makes no difference. For a discrete one it does.
  • Fill-in-the-blank sets: type the option number (like 8), not the value, unless the blank asks for a value.

Joint and conditional

  • Check your table adds to 1 before you use it.
  • Conditional PMF = row ÷ row total. It must also add to 1.
  • Range of (Y | X = x) = only the non-zero cells in that row.
  • \(f_{NX}(n,k)=0\) when k > n in Poisson splitting questions.
  • Split Poisson rate is λp, not λ.
  • Cards and balls: include the "everything else" factor in the numerator: \(\binom{40}{1}\) for the non-picture cards.
  • Chain rule: \(f_{XYZ}=f_{X\mid YZ}f_{YZ}\). Do not split \(f_{YZ}\) into \(f_Yf_Z\) unless told they are independent.

Independence, max, min

  • One matching cell proves nothing; one failing cell proves dependence.
  • A zero cell with non-zero row and column totals means dependent.
  • Max: P(max ≤ k) = product of P(Xᵢ ≤ k). Min: P(min ≥ k) = product of P(Xᵢ ≥ k).
  • P(max = k) = F(k)ⁿ − F(k − 1)ⁿ, not F(k)ⁿ.
  • "Exactly one" among n samples needs the factor n for which sample it is.
  • Sum of independent Poissons is Poisson; the conditional of one given the sum is Binomial with p = λ₁/(λ₁ + λ₂).

Expectation, variance, bounds

  • Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X). The +b disappears and a is squared.
  • Var(X − Y) = Var X + Var Y − 2 Cov; for independent variables it is a sum.
  • E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] only for independent variables. Cov = 0 does not mean independent.
  • ρ is between −1 and 1. If you get 1.3 you divided by a variance instead of an SD.
  • Markov needs X ≥ 0 and uses the mean. For integers, P(X > 50) = P(X ≥ 51).
  • Chebyshev uses σ² / c² with c the distance from the mean. For a lower bound, 1 − σ²/c².
  • Linearity works without replacement: expected prize from n coupons = n × (average prize per coupon).

CDF and PDF

  • PDF = derivative of the CDF; CDF = integral of the PDF from −∞.
  • Normalise with area, not height: the integral over the support must be 1. The PDF may exceed 1.
  • P(X = a) = jump in F at a; 0 for a continuous variable.
  • P(a < X ≤ b) = F(b) − F(a), left end open, right end closed.
  • Standardise with σ: z = (x − μ)/σ, and F_Z(−z) = 1 − F_Z(z).
  • Memoryless: exponential P(X > s + t | X > s) = e^(−λt); geometric P(X > m + n | X > m) = (1 − p)ⁿ.
